# What is NOT finished

This is important.

You have finished the **platform**.

You have **not** finished the **application**.

Those are different things.

Platform =
> "How should the system work?"

Application =
> "What features should users get?"

Now the future work becomes much easier.

# Overall assessment

If I compare your codebase to where it started—with direct SQL lookups scattered across the application—and where it is now, you've achieved a substantial architectural transformation.

You now have:

- A **Metadata subsystem** that owns entity loading and hydration.
- A **Search subsystem** that owns indexing and retrieval without knowing about UI DTOs.
- A **Membership subsystem** that owns containment and reverse lookups with caching and event-driven invalidation.
- Clear separation between renderer, IPC, services, repositories, and the database.

That's the kind of foundation that makes future features significantly easier to build because they compose existing services instead of duplicating logic.

I'd consider the **platform layer complete**, and I'd shift the roadmap from "building infrastructure" to "building capabilities on top of that infrastructure."
